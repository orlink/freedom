What is freedom?

We define freedom with respect to a specific individual or shared interest as the ability of people to self-govern, or, in other words, make and implement decisions without outside interference in respect to that interest.

People’s most fundamental interest is in the ability to form individual and shared interests.

People make and implement decisions in one of three ways: either directly, via representatives or via a selfless entity, such as a machine.

The established decision-making mechanisms include the General Assembly and the proper referendum, which has three main subtypes: the citizen initiative, the mandatory referendum and the veto-referendum.

A representative is a person or entity that is elected by the people and empowered to act on their behalf with respect to some interests of theirs and that can be held individually accountable by the represented. The last part is crucial. Someone making decisions for us, who we may have elected, but cannot hold accountable, is, by definition, a tyrant. Thus, a public representative should be elected by an electoral system, such as Single-Transferable-Vote that the prioritizes voting for individual candidates versus groups of candidates.

It follows that self-governing institutions should be organized around specific, shared or group interests, so as to prevent people or entities who do not have those interests, from directly or via representatives participate in the decision-making.

Thus, a large city municipal government, for example, should preferably not be empowered to make decisions regarding neighborhood-specific issues, such as playgrounds or walkways, because that would mean that city residents outside of a specific neighborhood, who don’t live in it and don’t normally use its amenities, would be making decisions for those, via their representatives in the municipal government. This is the curse of overly centralized government, which typically manifests in neglect or mismanagement of lower level interests.

By the same token, neighborhoods in a city should preferably have a form of self-government through which to manage their neighborhood-specific interests, which are not shared by residents in other neighborhoods in the municipalitiy, who may have similar but distinct interests of their own. And the neighborhood should definitely have at least one representative in the higher-level government, such as the municipal council, whose main job is to protect their neighborhood’s ability to self-govern, and who’s only second job is to participate in the governing of the municipal interests such as public transportation.
