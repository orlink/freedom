What is freedom?

Earlier plain-language version, kept for later review. Not linked from the menu. The current text is on What is freedom.

People are free if they possess the institutions and means of making and implementing decisions regarding their specific individual or shared interests without interference from outsiders.

Shared interests

People share interests at many levels: the entrance of an apartment building, the building, the neighbourhood (квартал), the village, town or city, the municipality, the county, the state, the country and a union such as the EU. Some shared interests are not tied to a place, such as a business or a non-profit organisation.

The people of a neighbourhood, for example, share an interest in its walkways, playgrounds and kindergartens. They are free in that respect if they can decide about these things themselves, without interference from people outside the neighbourhood who don't generally care about these neighbourhood-specific issues.

Direct and representative decisions

People make decisions either directly or through representatives. So the question for each level is: do the laws and regulations for that place allow its people to decide directly, through representatives, or both?

Directly, for example, through legally binding referendums on concrete proposals, with no turnout quorum and reasonable signature requirements, or through a general assembly.

Through representatives, for example, through a council that can make binding decisions. Representatives are real representatives only if the people can hold them individually accountable. That requires an electoral system in which people vote for each candidate individually. If a community has no council of its own, can it at least elect its own representative to the council above? Or is that council elected from party lists in a single district covering the whole municipality?

How the map shows it

Each level is a chip. Its colour shows how free its people are to govern that level's shared interests:

FullGoodWeakNone

Clicking a chip leads down the tree: the level's shared interests, then direct and representative decision-making, then the institutions (such as referendums and general assemblies), the properties each institution needs, and finally the laws that enable or prevent it.

The map is collaborative. Anyone can add a shared interest to a place, or mark one as not applicable there.
